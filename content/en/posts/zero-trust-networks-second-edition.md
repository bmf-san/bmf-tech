I read [Zero Trust Networks, 2nd Edition](https://link.amazon/B0hrLIOqG) (Japanese edition).

Razi Rais, Christina Morillo, Evan Gilman, and Doug Barth wrote this comprehensive guide to zero trust networks. Perimeter defense with firewalls and VPNs no longer holds up, so zero trust starts from the opposite premise: trust no connection and verify every one. The book shows how to bring that idea into system design and operations.

The early chapters cover trust management and authorization decisions. The book then goes through devices, identities, applications, and traffic, one chapter each, and explains how to establish trust in each of them. Each of these chapters ends with a scenario walkthrough that applies the ideas to a concrete case.

The second edition brings major updates, such as managing trust scores, practicing zero trust consistently from development through operations, and an overview of standards and guidelines. Later chapters also look at the attacker's perspective and at open challenges and the future of zero trust.

It suits engineers who want the big picture of how to build zero trust into the design of their systems.
