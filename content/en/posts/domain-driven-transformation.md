I read [Domain-Driven Transformation](https://link.amazon/B03N8gxD3) (Japanese edition).

Carola Lilienthal and Henning Schwentner offer a practical guide to rebuilding large, aging legacy systems as microservices or maintainable modular monoliths.

Domain-driven design (DDD) runs through the whole book, and the authors approach the work from three angles: strategy, technology, and organization. The foundation chapters explain collaborative modeling with Domain Storytelling and EventStorming, along with the Modularity Maturity Index (MMI), a measure of modularity. The strategic part lays out four steps: rediscover the domain, model a domain-driven target architecture, compare the current architecture with the target, and then decide which changes come first and carry them out.

The book favors steady, low-risk improvement over a full rewrite. The appendices catalog strategic, tactical, and sociotechnical refactorings, which makes them a handy reference during the actual work.

For architects and tech leads in charge of modernizing a legacy system, it offers one guide that spans strategy, technology, and organization.
