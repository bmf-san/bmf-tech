I read [Digital Identity: The Core of Cyber Business That Executives Don't Know](https://link.amazon/B0dRujVYY).

Natsuhiko Sakimura, one of the authors of the OpenID Connect Core 1.0 specification, wrote this Japanese book. His concern is that incidents in online businesses will keep happening as long as executives lack the knowledge at the core of those businesses.

According to the author, most companies build their business around applications or services. GAFA (Google, Apple, Facebook, and Amazon) instead put digital identity at the center, and he argues that this difference decided who won in cyber business. The book starts from there.

Over nine chapters, it moves from the basics of identity management to OpenID Connect as a framework for identity federation, then to access control and identity management from a company's point of view. The later chapters cover privacy protection, notice and consent in handling personal data, and trust frameworks.

The book targets executives rather than engineers. Still, engineers who work on authentication and authorization can rely on it when they need to explain why digital identity sits at the center of a business.
