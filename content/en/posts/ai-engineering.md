I read [AI Engineering: Building Applications with Foundation Models](https://link.amazon/B07qDWn14) (Japanese edition).

Chip Huyen's book covers how to design, build, and run AI applications on top of foundation models such as large language models. It goes beyond calling a model and treats reliability and safety as part of the job.

Its ten chapters move from how foundation models work to evaluation, prompt engineering, RAG and agents, fine-tuning, dataset engineering, inference optimization, and finally architecture and user feedback.

Two whole chapters focus on evaluation. They run from metrics such as perplexity to AI as a judge, ranking models through comparative evaluation, and how to choose a model. The outputs of foundation models vary from run to run, so this focus on evaluation makes sense to me.

For engineers who build products with generative AI, it works as a map that shows where each technique fits.
