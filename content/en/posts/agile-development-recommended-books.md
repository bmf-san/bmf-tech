A staged reading list for anyone learning agile and lean development, from the basics to real practice. Each pick has its own review on this blog, and every entry links to it. The order follows a learning path: agile basics, then practice and improvement, and lean validation of value.

## Why learn agile development

When requirements and markets keep shifting, the plan-it-once-and-build-it-all assumption breaks down. Learn to work in short loops and improve as you go, and you grow resilient to uncertainty. Grasp the thinking behind the process, and you avoid adopting it in name only.

## 1. Get started with agile

### The Agile Samurai

A classic that conveys the whole picture of agile in plain language. You get a map of where to begin. I cover it in the [review](/posts/agile-samurai/).

### The Scrum Boot Camp Book

It teaches the basics of Scrum along the motions of a real team. You grasp the terms and flow through a concrete picture. I wrote it up in the [review](/posts/scrum-boot-camp-agile-development/).

## 2. Practice and improve agile

### Kaizen Journey

It tells the story of spreading agile in the field. It gives you the courage to start alone and draw others in. My notes are in the [review](/posts/kaizen-journey/).

### More Effective Agile

It gathers broad, practical wisdom for turning agile into results. It helps when adoption stalls. See the [review](/posts/more-effective-agile/).

### Building the Right Thing the Right Way

It presses you to rethink what to build and face the product. It suits anyone looking past agile itself. I cover it in the [review](/posts/building-correctly/).

## 3. Validate value with lean

### Running Lean

It lays out the lean-startup steps of forming a hypothesis and testing it fast. You cut wasted effort and maximize learning. I wrote it up in the [review](/posts/running-lean-practical-startup/).

### Lean UX

It brings lean thinking into user-experience design. You build the habit of testing before you build. My notes are in the [review](/posts/lean-ux-user-experience-design/).

### Lean Analytics

It explains, in a systematic way, how startups use data to decide. You learn to pick metrics and turn them into learning. See the [review](/posts/lean-analytics-startup-data-analysis/).

## 4. Toward sustainable development

### Simplicity

It considers sustainable, humane development through the lens of simplicity. You gain a reason to pause before chasing speed. I cover it in the [review](/posts/simplicity/).

## Wrap-up

Start with the two primers to get a map of agile, then firm up your footing with practice and improvement. Go deep on lean and sustainability once what you build comes into view. Every book has a full review here, so start with whichever one interests you most.
