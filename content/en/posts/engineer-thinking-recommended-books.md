A reading list for anyone who wants to sharpen their thinking and intellectual productivity as an engineer. Each pick has its own review on this blog, and every entry links to it. The books span problem solving, logical thinking and communication, abstraction, and how to learn.

## Why learn how to think

Technology comes and goes, but the ability to define a problem and reason through it carries across every field. With patterns of thought at hand, you face complex problems calmly. Sharpen how you learn, and your growth speeds up too.

## 1. Solve and frame problems

### How to Solve It

A classic in which the mathematician Polya lays out a universal procedure for problem solving. You learn how to find footholds for thinking. I cover it in the [review](/posts/solving-problems/).

### Start from the Issue (Revised Edition)

It argues for the value of spotting the question truly worth solving. You build the habit of narrowing the point before pouring in effort. My notes are in the [review](/posts/start-with-issues-revised/).

## 2. Think and communicate logically

### Logical Thinking

A staple on reasoning in order and conveying it clearly. You pick up patterns like MECE and structuring. I wrote it up in the [review](/posts/logical-thinking/).

### Logical Thinking and Writing

It brings the conclusion-first pyramid structure into everyday practice. You learn to assemble writing that lands. See the [review](/posts/logical-thinking-techniques-for-japanese/).

## 3. Abstraction and the craft of thought

### Concrete and Abstract

It shows how moving between the concrete and the abstract shapes the quality of thought. Your grasp of things deepens a level. I cover it in the [review](/posts/concrete-abstract-intelligence/).

### The Art of Organizing Thought

A classic on the intellectual habits of resting and growing ideas. You rethink the process of shaping thoughts. My notes are in the [review](/posts/organizing-thoughts/).

### Purpose-Driven Thinking

It treats a stance of reasoning from purpose rather than drifting to means. Your priorities gain a backbone. I wrote it up in the [review](/posts/purpose-driven-thinking/).

## 4. How to learn and produce

### An Engineer's Intellectual Productivity

It treats, in a systematic way, how to learn, organize, and output well. You design your own learning cycle. See the [review](/posts/engineer-productive-learning/).

### How to Read a Book

A classic that splits reading into four levels and teaches how to read deeply. It changes how you approach technical books too. I cover it in the [review](/posts/book-reading-guide/).

### The Thinking Methods of World-Class Engineers

It shares habits of thought and work honed in top-tier settings. It offers hints for rethinking how you go about your day. My notes are in the [review](/posts/thinking-like-top-engineers/).

## Wrap-up

Start by building a base with problem solving and logical thinking. Widen into abstraction and learning as your interest leads. Every book has a full review here, so start with whichever one interests you most.
