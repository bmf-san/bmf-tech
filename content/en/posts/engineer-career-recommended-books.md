A reading list for anyone thinking about their career as a software engineer. Each pick has its own review on this blog, and every entry links to it. The list starts with the fundamentals of the job, then moves to how to approach your career and the path to technical leadership.

## Why read about your career

An engineering career is not a straight staircase but a series of decisions about job changes, promotions, and shifting roles. Knowing how others thought through those moments gives you a frame for your own decisions. It also helps you spot the skills you lack and decide what to grow next.

## 1. Build the fundamentals

### Fundamentals of Software Engineering

It bridges the gap between writing programs and working as a software engineer. From reading and writing code, testing, and deploying and monitoring, to learning and career management, it lays out the whole job. I cover it in the [review](/posts/fundamentals-of-software-engineering/).

## 2. How to approach your career

### Being Geek

A book on career strategy for staying a geek. Its voice has quirks, yet it offers plenty to learn about how an engineer works and thinks. See the [review](/posts/being-geek-career-strategy/).

### The Software Developer's Career Handbook

By Michael Lopp, the author of Being Geek, it helps you move through career uncertainty. For job changes, promotions, and working with your manager, it offers questions to guide your judgment instead of one right answer. I wrote it up in the [review](/posts/software-developers-career-handbook/).

## 3. The path to technical leadership

### The Staff Engineer's Path

A compass for engineers who lead teams and organizations. It covers how to set a vision and strategy and how to act as a leader, which also helps anyone aiming at an architect or CTO role. I cover it in the [review](/posts/staff-engineer-guide/).

## Wrap-up

Early in your career, read Fundamentals of Software Engineering to see the whole job. When you feel stuck, turn to Being Geek and The Software Developer's Career Handbook; when you want to lead through technology, The Staff Engineer's Path shows the way. Every book has a full review here, so start with whichever one interests you most.
