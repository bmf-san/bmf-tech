A staged reading list for anyone learning engineering management, from building organizations to leadership and product management. Each pick has its own review on this blog, and every entry links to it. The order moves from organization and team design to the manager's role, technical strategy, and product.

## Why learn engineering management

Team results ride on more than individual skill; they depend on how you shape the organization and the quality of management. With a systematic grounding, you decide without leaning on gut feel alone. Understanding both technology and management widens your career.

## 1. Design organizations and teams

### An Invitation to Engineering Organization Theory

It unpacks the link between thinking and organizations through the lens of uncertainty. It builds the foundation that management rests on. I cover it in the [review](/posts/invitation-to-engineering-organization/).

### Team Topologies

It organizes team boundaries and relationships into four types and three interaction modes. You come to see organization structure alongside software design. I wrote it up in the [review](/posts/team-topologies-adaptive-organization-design/).

### Dynamic Reteaming, 2nd Edition

It frames team change as five recurring patterns. You gain a way to design reorganizations with intent. My notes are in the [review](/posts/dynamic-re-teaming-second-edition/).

## 2. The manager's and leader's role

### The Engineering Manager's Job

It lays out an engineering manager's work grounded in day-to-day practice. It suits a new manager's first read. I cover it in the [review](/posts/engineering-manager-role-guide/).

### Leading Engineering Teams

Drawing on Google's culture, it covers leading teams from both IC and manager angles. It helps anyone raise their influence, whatever their title. I wrote it up in the [review](/posts/engineering-team-leadership-guide/).

### The Senior Engineering Leader's Job

It portrays a leader's role across a wider span of responsibility. It helps you look toward the next step past management. My notes are in the [review](/posts/senior-engineering-leader/).

### The Leader's Etiquette

It argues that small, consistent behaviors build trust. It nudges you to revisit your daily words and actions. See the [review](/posts/leader-etiquette/).

## 3. The CTO and technical strategy

### The CTO's Way of Thinking

It treats decisions that move technology and organizations through strategy and leadership. You gain judgment for roles close to the business. I cover it in the [review](/posts/cto-thinking-strategy-leadership/).

### How to Build an Engineering Strategy

It shows concretely how to set and run a technical organization's strategy. It moves you past ad-hoc decisions. I wrote it up in the [review](/posts/engineering-strategy/).

## 4. Change the organization

### Five Dialogues to Change Your Organization

It covers growing an agile culture through conversation. You learn patterns of communication that move people and teams. My notes are in the [review](/posts/five-dialogues-for-organizational-change/).

## 5. Product management

### Escaping the Build Trap

It teaches how to focus on value and avoid making shipping an end in itself. It moves you toward product-minded management. I cover it in the [review](/posts/escaping-the-build-trap/).

### Product Management in Practice, 2nd Edition

A broad, practical guide to a product manager's work you can use from day one. It suits anyone who wants the whole picture of the role. I wrote it up in the [review](/posts/product-management-in-practice-second-edition/).

## Wrap-up

Start with organization theory and team design, then move to the role closest to your own. Go deep on technical strategy and product management as your span of responsibility grows. Every book has a full review here, so start with whichever one interests you most.
