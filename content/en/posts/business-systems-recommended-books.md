A reading list for anyone who wants to learn the basics of business systems from an engineer's view, covering accounting, inventory, and logistics. Each pick has its own review on this blog, and every entry links to it. All belong to the "Knowledge and Skills of X Systems for Engineers" series, which pairs the background of the work with how the system supports it.

## Why learn about business systems

When you build business systems, understanding the target domain shapes quality as much as technology does. Know the basics of a domain like accounting or inventory, and you design with the reasons behind the requirements. Engineers who bridge business and systems earn their keep in the field.

## 1. Accounting and finance

### Knowledge and Skills of Accounting Systems for Engineers

It explains accounting basics like bookkeeping and journal entries from a system-building view. You build a base before you touch an accounting system. I cover it in the [review](/posts/accounting-system-knowledge-skills/).

### Knowledge and Technology of Financial Systems for Engineers

It treats how financial operations work and the key points of the systems behind them. You grasp the whole of a highly specialized area. My notes are in the [review](/posts/financial-system-knowledge-skills/).

## 2. Inventory, production, and logistics

### Knowledge and Skills of Inventory Management Systems for Engineers

It pairs the ideas of inventory management with how a system handles them. You learn the key points of holding down shortage and surplus. I wrote it up in the [review](/posts/inventory-management-knowledge-skills/).

### Knowledge and Skills of Production Management Systems for Engineers

It covers production management from the basics to the key points of building a system. You understand the machinery behind the manufacturing floor. See the [review](/posts/knowledge-and-skills-in-production-management-for-engineers/).

### Knowledge and Technology of Logistics Systems for Engineers

It explains the flow of logistics and the role of the systems that support it. You come to see the machinery behind moving goods. I cover it in the [review](/posts/logistics-systems-knowledge-and-skills-for-engineers/).

## Wrap-up

Start from the title closest to the work you touch. Once you hold the domain basics, your requirements and design gain precision. Every book has a full review here, so start with whichever one interests you most.
