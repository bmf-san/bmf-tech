I read [Platform Engineering](https://link.amazon/B05qEehFD) (Japanese edition).

Camille Fournier and Ian Nowland wrote this book about building and running a shared platform that helps developers create software efficiently.

The book has three parts. Part I explains why platform engineering matters now and lays out its pillars. Part II covers the practice: the platform as a product, operations, planning and delivery, re-architecture, migrations and sunsetting, and stakeholder relationships. Part III turns to success and gives one chapter to each of four goals: alignment, trust, managed complexity, and a platform that users love.

What stood out to me is how consistently the authors treat the platform as a product. They see internal developers as customers and go as far as product discovery and roadmaps. I also like that a whole chapter deals with migrations and sunsetting, which matches what real platform work looks like.

It suits platform team leads and product managers, and it also helps organizations still deciding whether they need a platform at all.
