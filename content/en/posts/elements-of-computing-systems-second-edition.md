I read [The Elements of Computing Systems, Second Edition: Building a Modern Computer from First Principles](https://link.amazon/B0cpvUobH) (Japanese edition).

Noam Nisan and Shimon Schocken's bestseller, known as Nand to Tetris, teaches how computers work by having you build one from scratch. This is its second edition.

You start from the NAND gate and design logic gates, adders, and a CPU. From there you write an assembler, a virtual machine, a compiler, and an OS. In the end, applications such as Tetris run on the computer you built.

Because you build every layer from the hardware up to the OS yourself, you get a hands-on feel for how the layers connect. An appendix also explains the online IDE, so you can work through the projects in a browser.

It suits engineers who want to tie their scattered low-level knowledge into one connected picture.
