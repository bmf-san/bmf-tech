A reading list for engineers who want to learn how to form a strategy and how to see problems as structure. Each pick has its own review on this blog, and every entry links to it. The list starts with strategy, then moves through systems thinking, critical thinking and problem discovery, and how to ask questions.

## Why learn strategy and systems thinking

Technical decisions are strategy too: you choose where to concentrate limited resources. Systems thinking turns your attention from the symptom in front of you to the structure that produces it. Knowing which problem to solve shapes your results more than how fast you solve it.

## 1. Learn strategy

### Good Strategy, Bad Strategy

It argues that a good strategy contains a guide to action, built on a diagnosis, a guiding policy, and coherent actions. You also learn why deciding what not to do matters, and how bad strategy mistakes goals for strategy. I cover it in the [review](/posts/good-strategy-bad-strategy/).

### The Crux

Rumelt's follow-up to Good Strategy, Bad Strategy. It goes deeper into diagnosis: finding the crux, the one point in a tangle of challenges that moves everything once you solve it. See the [review](/posts/the-crux-strategy/).

## 2. See structure with systems thinking

### Thinking in Systems

Donella Meadows' classic on systems thinking. Through stocks and flows, feedback loops, and delays, it teaches you to look for the cause of a problem in the structure itself. I wrote it up in the [review](/posts/thinking-in-systems/).

### Into the World of Systems Thinking

It shows how software engineers can bring systems thinking into their work. It introduces the core tools of systems thinking and ties them to what happens in software development and organizations. I cover it in the [review](/posts/thinking-in-systems-for-software-engineers/).

## 3. Thinking and problem discovery

### Globis MBA Critical Thinking

A book that teaches critical thinking through exercises. It suits you when you want the habit of checking your own reasoning. See the [review](/posts/globis-mba-critical-thinking/).

### Are Your Lights On?

A classic whose message is that finding the problem is harder than solving it. It reminds you that if you misjudge the problem, even a brilliant solution is worthless. I wrote it up in the [review](/posts/problem-discovery-human-science/).

## 4. Ask better questions

### Designing Questions

A book on facilitating creative dialogue: how to frame questions and how to draw them out. It makes you feel how directly the quality of a question drives the quality of the outcome. I cover it in the [review](/posts/designing-questions-facilitation/).

## Wrap-up

Start with Good Strategy, Bad Strategy to grasp the skeleton of strategy, then deepen your diagnosis with The Crux. For structural thinking, begin with Thinking in Systems, and widen your view with the books on finding problems and asking questions. Every book has a full review here, so start with whichever one interests you most.
